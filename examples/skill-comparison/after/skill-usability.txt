Independent forward-test observations

The revised design workflow supported a successful new composed room. The strongest practical guidance was to plan the after-use state with the before-use state, distinguish geometry from reachable pushing faces, and use successful bypasses to revise claims. Four failed/bypassed candidates led to specific fixes rather than extra decorative obstacles.

1. The standard transport builder and verifier work with arbitrary authored cells and role assignments, including this new elbow/keeper/target geometry. Build output and the ordinary replay make the result concrete and reviewable. Unspecified coordinates were treated as design choices throughout.

2. Composition has an expected verification gap: boundary-stagger's checker supports exactly two groups, whereas the composed room needs three and void. The guidance correctly warns against claiming broader checker coverage, but a combined author has to implement scoped boundary prefixes and wall controls. A generic reusable API or CLI for directional/prefix/wall checks would reduce this work. The supplied runtime module is useful for this adaptation.

3. The distinction between movement necessity and order was useful. A naive first-M0-R endpoint would accept an R that merely undoes the initial L retreat. The actual boundary claim needed a progress endpoint (minimum M0 x > 3 OR gem). A recipe showing this undo-to-initial pitfall could improve the planning guidance.

4. The transport verifier's exact declared delivery is intentionally narrower than all usable contact opportunities. Attempt 4 solved through a higher tool pose while bypassing recovery, so changing only its contract would have concealed a failed design dependency. The revised instructions helped separate that counterexample from a false unsolvability diagnosis.

5. The existing boundary browser script is not directly reusable with transport output: it assumes a manifest map field and contract phases. I supplied phases and used a small local wrapper for Play replay, Undo/Reset, editor rendering, and export. A transport-compatible browser helper could avoid this routine adaptation.

6. Official moves can remove a player or group after a fall into void. Early custom exploratory direction/reachability code incorrectly assumed every successful move retained an actor. Those local scripts were corrected, and their failed records retained. This was a custom-checker edge case, not a demonstrated defect in the skill runtime. A reachability example that filters living z0 players would be useful.

Remaining limits are explicit: no human playtest, no shortest-solution claim, no global shape-minimality proof, no exhaustive deadlock audit, and no claim that one witness's precise parking pose or compass sequence is unique. Successful optional-direction and altered-wall routes are retained rather than hidden.
