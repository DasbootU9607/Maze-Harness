# Goal-derived cross geometry

Start with collecting the gem and arriving alive at the declared doorway. The doorway is an in-room cell, not a removed player or a nonexistent neighbor transition.

Derive the final arrival ray and its turning stop. Identify the member that brakes the incoming slide and the wall or floor transition ending the outgoing slide. Intermediate transit cells are not stopping cells; gem collection can occur in transit, so inspect actual move records.

For each proposed input declare the stance, contacted member, full before/after footprints, and member/terrain pair limiting travel. An arm can hit a wall before the contacted member does. Support and clearance apply to the whole body throughout the slide; the official engine decides where it and the player stop.

Find a preceding pose that makes the next stance reachable by directed sliding with the cross frozen. Record a stance route and try the ordinary input. Adjacency, open floor or a passed-over square is not usable access. Account for entry and retreat after every move.

Temporary parking should enable a new pushing side or brake while preserving a later restoration route. Opposing inputs and returns to an earlier pose count only when the intermediate arrangement enables necessary access.

Assign arms concrete input, clearance, endpoint or braking functions. The packaged profile allows a connected four-armed cross with positive integer arm lengths at z=0. Removing an arm also changes collision/access; analyze matched interventions separately and do not claim global minimality from one changed layout.

Choose new coordinates from inherited functions. Recheck collision pairs, directed stopping graphs, next stances, restrictions and the complete return route; do not paste the reference's pose events into an unrelated map.
