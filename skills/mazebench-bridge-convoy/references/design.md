# Designing a Bridge Convoy puzzle

1. Define a complete destination and any surviving-tool condition. A zero-gem solved flag is insufficient for a passage puzzle.
2. Draw a region-access graph. Mark irreversible floor placements and what stance, tool, or passage each placement enables. Retain a floor for a later requirement only if the route actually delivers it there.
3. Assign the gate occupant and bridge-producing front member. Work backward from the next useful contact to supported player access.
4. Construct staging and recovery space for both independent modules. Show how the player crosses the boundary while each module remains recoverable.
5. Design the turn with ordinary translations. Show the before/after actor order, every side stance, complete collision clearance and support. The post-turn chain must have a legal useful push.
6. Let bridge consumption produce a route that is used immediately or in the later endpoint route. Test that the reusable module arrives usable and survives as required.
7. Replay the full ordinary route; identify observed relations and test relevant bypasses with compatible search or carefully scoped controls. A cap stays unknown.

Use the empty design template, filling its mechanism reading receipt only after reading the current mechanism document. The reference's exact actors, coordinates and route belong only to regression evidence. A composed design connects planning, gate access, recovery, turn and delivery; a requested compact example may isolate one relation with an explicit limited objective. No fixed number of stages or modules is inherited from another skill.

The supplied verifier is tied to the official BxE regression. A new layout needs its own builder and observer. Do not rename a transformed reference and call it transfer validation.
