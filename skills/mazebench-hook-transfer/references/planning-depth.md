# Planning before and after contact

Before laying out a default complex room, read [Mechanism logic](mechanism-logic.md), this file, and the [contract and verification interfaces](complex-validation.md), then use the complex entrypoints. Earlier examples provide evidence only within their declared scope.

For default complex authoring, make recognition, preparation, usable contact, and continued access depend on one another. These are design criteria, not a calibrated human-difficulty scale.

| Stage | Design question | What must remain usable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognize a tool | Why cannot the player make the useful target move directly? | Working part, support, and visible target restriction |
| Prepare and dock | Which relative positions must change, and what blocks a naive approach? | Full tool footprint and a reachable input stance |
| Apply contact | Which contact transmits the push, and what restriction changes? | Target pose, player escape, and the next pushing face |
| Complete the task | What still blocks the route or a necessary operation? | Withdrawal, further rearrangement, or another verified approach |

Design these stages together. A shared bay may be needed for docking and later for player circulation. A displaced target can change the next available pushing face. A tool can solve one obstruction while becoming another that must be removed. Each extra phase needs a concrete spatial conflict; distance alone is insufficient.

Derive P, I, C, T, and d at contact and inspect the entire footprint along the approach. If a supposed phase is avoidable, revise the actual relationship or narrow the claim instead of imposing a compass sequence.

## Test the planning claim

- Use the real objective. A passage task without a gem needs an explicit arrival condition, not an empty-gem success check.
- Separate completion, qualifying contact, contact necessity, and preparation necessity. Use the [first-event method](verification.md#first-event-prefix-search) for preparation before use.
- Freeze claimed necessary objects or exclude named directions. Replay any complete bypass rather than hiding it.
- Test continuation from recorded contact states. Walking-only failure from one state does not establish that failure after every possible first-contact state.
- Declare direct tool input versus a relay. An indirect contact chain may be a legitimate different mechanism requiring a different observer.
- Explain a compact sequence of functional changes alongside the full route. Repeated pushes and search expansions are not difficulty scores.

For human review, show the unplayed room first. Observe whether players identify the useful working part, anticipate the required stance, and plan beyond contact. Keep hints, failed plans, and completion observations separate from engine correctness.
